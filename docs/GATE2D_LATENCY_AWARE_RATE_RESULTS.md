# Gate 2D: Latency-aware latent-token rate control

## Question

Can the 0.8 ms failure found in Gate 2C be repaired by changing only the
latent coordination-token precision, without altering the learned message
content, Top-1 target mask, movement actor, power split, structured evidence
codec or detector threshold?

All results use the selection20-only standardized detector threshold and the
same stress20 geometries.

## Frozen rate intervention

The established deterministic policy transmits every active latent target
token at 8 bit/dimension. An evaluation-only intervention replaces that rate
with the lowest active configured rate, 4 bit/dimension. It leaves all actor
parameters and token values unchanged before quantization.

| Condition | Rate | steady | weak3 | worst | CVaR | QoS | Latent delivery | Total bit/frame |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Nominal | 8 | 0.8886 | 0.8514 | 0.6379 | 0.0418 | 0.55 | 1.000 | 1019.5 |
| Nominal | 4 | 0.8877 | 0.8503 | 0.6331 | 0.0413 | 0.50 | 1.000 | 766.1 |
| SNR threshold 30 dB | 8 | 0.8868 | 0.8491 | 0.6308 | 0.0418 | 0.55 | 1.000 | 1019.5 |
| SNR threshold 30 dB | 4 | 0.8857 | 0.8477 | 0.6252 | 0.0413 | 0.50 | 1.000 | 766.1 |
| SNR threshold 35 dB | 8 | 0.8646 | 0.8194 | 0.5553 | 0.0229 | 0.50 | 0.812 | 1009.1 |
| SNR threshold 35 dB | 4 | 0.8610 | 0.8147 | 0.5370 | 0.0214 | 0.45 | 0.811 | 749.9 |
| Deadline 0.8 ms | 8 | 0.8264 | 0.7685 | 0.4308 | 0.0175 | 0.40 | 0.535 | 998.9 |
| Deadline 0.8 ms | 4 | 0.8876 | 0.8501 | 0.6348 | 0.0434 | 0.55 | 1.000 | 766.3 |
| Deadline 0.4 ms | 8 | 0.7910 | 0.7213 | 0.3342 | 0.0147 | 0.25 | 0.000 | 998.4 |
| Deadline 0.4 ms | 4 | 0.8082 | 0.7443 | 0.3684 | 0.0179 | 0.25 | 0.023 | 737.3 |

At nominal conditions, 4 bit reduces total traffic by 253.5 bit/frame
(24.9%) while its paired worst difference from 8 bit is `-0.00484`, with a
95% bootstrap interval of `[-0.01372, 0.00146]`.

At 0.8 ms, 4 bit raises latent-token delivery by 46.5 percentage points and
improves mean worst by `+0.20392`, with a 95% interval of
`[0.08000, 0.34109]`. It restores all three Medium average thresholds.

The intervention does not repair the 35 dB SNR outage because quantization
rate does not change received SNR. It also cannot make 0.4 ms physically
deployable because almost every latent packet still misses that deadline.

## Bounded transport accounting fix

The first randomized-rate training smoke exposed an independent reward-scale
bug. When communication power approached zero, the Shannon serialization time
of an undelivered packet became extremely large. The environment:

1. kept transmitting that already-expired packet for its full theoretical
   serialization time when calculating energy; and
2. charged the uncapped theoretical latency again in the reward.

This produced a mean reward near `-3.8e12` and critic loss near `5.7e13`.

The corrected model aborts failed transmissions at the configured deadline:
raw latency remains available as a diagnostic, but RF energy is billed for at
most one deadline and the reward latency term is capped at that deadline.
The missed-deadline indicator remains a separate penalty.

After the correction, the same one-update smoke has:

- critic loss `146.7`;
- mean return `294.3`;
- finite communication and evidence costs;
- PPO old/new log-probability consistency error `8e-6`.

Successful-packet transport and all zero-training evaluation metrics remain
unchanged.

## Channel-randomized adaptive-rate screen

Only the categorical rate head is trained; every movement, message-content,
attention, commitment, matching and sensing-resource parameter is frozen.
Active rates all retain Top-1 cardinality. Episode-level training profiles are
nominal, 30 dB, 35 dB and 0.8 ms. Profile sampling uses a cloned RNG state, so
it does not change the geometry generated by a fixed seed.

The old explicit 8-bit bonus is disabled. The calibrated binary QoS critic is
loaded as a CTDE diagnostic, while the uncalibrated quantile/CVaR loss remains
disabled.

After four updates the deterministic rate policy uses approximately half
4-bit and half 8-bit actions:

| Condition | Method | steady | weak3 | worst | CVaR | QoS | Latent delivery | Total bit/frame |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Nominal | Fixed 4 bit | 0.8877 | 0.8503 | 0.6331 | 0.0413 | 0.50 | 1.000 | 766.1 |
| Nominal | Adaptive | 0.8887 | 0.8516 | 0.6374 | 0.0458 | 0.55 | 1.000 | 892.3 |
| 0.8 ms | Fixed 4 bit | 0.8876 | 0.8501 | 0.6348 | 0.0434 | 0.55 | 1.000 | 766.3 |
| 0.8 ms | Adaptive | 0.8544 | 0.8059 | 0.5291 | 0.0324 | 0.40 | 0.735 | 889.5 |

At nominal conditions, adaptive-minus-fixed-4 worst is `+0.00436`, with
95% interval `[-0.00213, 0.01335]`. At 0.8 ms it is `-0.10569`, with interval
`[-0.23291, -0.00102]`. The adaptive route therefore fails the predeclared
robustness direction gate and is not expanded to more seeds or longer
training.

## Decision

The fixed 4-bit latent stream is the current deployable operating point:

- it meets steady, weak3 and average-worst requirements at nominal, 30 dB and
  0.8 ms conditions;
- it reduces traffic relative to the original learned 8-bit action;
- it does not claim tail reliability, 35 dB robustness, 0.4 ms feasibility or
  scale generalization.

The four-update adaptive checkpoint is diagnostic only. Team-level delayed
credit is still unable to prevent sender-specific unsafe 8-bit choices under
the tight deadline.

The next admissible adaptive method must expose locally measurable channel
feedback (ACK history, measured SNR, latency margin and age of information) to
the rate head and train a sender-specific counterfactual delivery/value
advantage. Further reward-weight tuning or longer training of the current
hidden-channel-state head is not justified.
